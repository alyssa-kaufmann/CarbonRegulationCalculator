Your company operates in California and makes over $500 million annually:

You are required to begin measuring and tracking your Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions. You should establish accounting systems now to monitor these emissions and prepare for future compliance obligations. While Scope 3 emissions are not currently mandatory at this revenue level, you should stay informed about regulatory changes, as California's requirements are expanding.

Your company operates in California and makes over $1 billion annually:

You must publicly disclose your Scope 1 and Scope 2 greenhouse gas emissions every year. Additionally, you are required to disclose your Scope 3 emissions starting by January 1, 2026. You must also report on climate-related financial risks and their potential impact on your business, maintain detailed documentation of all emissions calculations, and obtain third-party verification of your emissions data before public reporting

Your company operates in New York City with a building over 25,000 square feet:

Your building is subject to New York City's Local Law 97, which requires you to meet specific emissions intensity benchmarks. You must measure and report your building's annual greenhouse gas emissions (Scope 1 and 2) and ensure they do not exceed the emissions intensity limits set by the city. These limits have been phased in over time, with stricter targets in effect as of 2024. You are required to submit annual emissions reports to the city, and failure to meet the benchmarks may result in significant financial penalties.

Your company operates in New York (state) and emits over 25,000 metric tons of CO2 a year in New York:

Your facility is subject to New York State's Mandatory Greenhouse Gas Emissions Reporting Program (GGERV). You are required to measure, calculate, and annually report your Scope 1 emissions and Scope 2 emissions. You must maintain detailed documentation of all emissions data, calculation methodologies, and supporting records for verification purposes. Failure to report or significant underreporting of emissions may result in penalties and enforcement action by the state.

Federal Laws:

In March of 2024 the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) released a new framework requring all public companies to disclose their Scope 1 and Scope 2 emmissions annually. However due to legal challenges, this law was never fully enforced. In May 2026, the SEC formally proposed repealing this framework. Thus, there is no active federal regulations around emmissions disclosures.
